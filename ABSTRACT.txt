\begin{abstract}

Open source modeling has emerged as a transformative paradigm in energy and power systems studies by enabling transparent, reproducible and collaborative development of models and tools. However, limited access to high-resolution, localized data reduces model accuracy, especially in regions with infrastructural constraints and complex weather conditions. This paper introduces a novel, systematic Open Source Modeling Method for Energy and Power Systems (OSM-MEPS). 
Derived and synthesized from foundational steps outlined in modeling literature, OSM-MEPS is grounded in first-principles thinking, the scientific method and the engineering design process frameworks, ensuring technical rigor, empirical validation and adaptability. To demonstrate effectiveness, OSM-MEPS guided the modeling and simulation of a solar photovoltaic (PV) system using high-resolution weather and irradiance data for the year 2024 from Solcast-DNV. 
At the Serres-C site in Greece, OSM-MEPS model results achieved a strong fit with real-world solar PV energy data (MAE: 319.71 kWh, RMSE: 406.76 kWh, $R^2$: 0.945), outperforming PVLIB (MAE: 378.52 kWh, RMSE: 456.24 kWh, $R^2$: 0.931). Seasonal testing showed similar results: in May 2024, the OSM-MEPS model achieved $R^2$: 0.927 vs. PVLIB’s 0.855; in December, it maintained $R^2$: 0.935 vs. PVLIB’s 0.888. Geographic validation demonstrated generalizability. 
In Pretoria, the OSM-MEPS model outperformed PVLIB ($R^2$: 0.686 vs. 0.297); In Adelaide, OSM-MEPS performed better ($R^2$: 0.866), compared to PVLIB ($R^2$: 0.7850). In Kisumu, both models had low $R^2$, due to energy data gaps and limited shading metadata for modeling. A rooftop energy feasibility study was carried out in Njagu-Mhasibu-Ruiru Estate in Kenya, based on a solar PV installed capacity of 8.75 kW. 
The OSM-MEPS model predicted an annual yield of 14,426 kWh/year, compared to PVLIB’s estimate of 13,787 kWh/year and the GSA reference of 12,784 kWh/year. Overall, the results confirm that the OSM-MEPS method and its resulting model are robust and adaptable tools for high-resolution solar PV energy modeling across diverse geographic regions and weather conditions.

\end{abstract}
