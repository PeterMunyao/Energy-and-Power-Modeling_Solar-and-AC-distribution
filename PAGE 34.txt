Statistical evaluation, summarized in Table~\ref{tab:model_performance} shows that the OSM-MEPS model closely matched the measured energy output at the Serres-C site in Greece, achieving a mean absolute error (MAE) of 319.71~kWh, a root mean squared error (RMSE) of 406.76~kWh, and an $R^2$ of 0.945 over the full year. 
In contrast, PVLIB recorded a higher MAE of 378.52~kWh, RMSE of 456.24~kWh and a lower $R^2$ of 0.931. Seasonal analysis further confirmed OSM-MEPS’s robustness: in May~2024, it achieved an $R^2$ of 0.927, outperforming PVLIB’s 0.855, with lower MAE (351.08~kWh vs.\ 518.94~kWh) and RMSE (459.59~kWh vs.\ 650.02~kWh). 
In December~2024, OSM-MEPS maintained better accuracy (MAE~=~255.39~kWh, RMSE~=~349.75~kWh, $R^2=0.935$) compared to PVLIB (MAE~=~348.91~kWh, RMSE~=~460.04~kWh, $R^2=0.888$).

Geographic tests showed similar trends. In Pretoria, OSM-MEPS achieved a MAE of 4.54~kWh, RMSE of 5.91~kWh and $R^2=0.686$, while PVLIB showed higher errors and a lower $R^2=0.297$. 
In Adelaide, PVLIB recorded a MAE of 7.91~kWh, RMSE of 8.70~kWh, and $R^2=0.785$, whereas OSM-MEPS performed better ($R^2=0.866$, MAE~=~5.16~kWh, RMSE~=~6.86~kWh). 
In Kisumu, PVLIB showed a negative $R^2=-0.073$, with MAE~=~0.86~kWh and RMSE~=~1.51~kWh, while OSM-MEPS achieved $R^2=0.047$, MAE~=~1.04~kWh, and RMSE~=~1.42~kWh. 
These errors were partly influenced by data limitations, key in modeling, such as incomplete annual records and logging issues reported on PVOutput~\cite{PVOutput2025}. 
Medium shading was noted in metadata without temporal detail and unreported soiling, maintenance downtime, or atmospheric smoke from refuse burning added further uncertainty.

The results highlight OSM-MEPS’s high accuracy in capturing daily and seasonal dynamics across diverse climates. 
Its performance edge is most evident during high-variability months and in non-temperate regions, demonstrating geographic generalizability. While PVLIB remains accessible for basic modeling, its deterministic structure and lack of integrated empirical loss adjustments limit its forecasting reliability. 
These findings underscore the importance of thermal coupling in solar PV module design, advanced irradiance modeling and loss characterization for improving photovoltaic output simulations.
